## Where we start the week

The Nasdaq-100 enters Monday roughly **9.5% below its 2 June record**, just inside correction
territory, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index is already in a **bear market** — down more
than 20% from its high. Chip equities shed **over $1trn** in the late-July selloff. Samsung and
SK hynix each fell **more than 15% in a single session**.

Here is the fact that should shape how you read everything else this week: **memory contract
prices went up while all of that happened.** Third-quarter DRAM contracts settled higher again,
and 2026 HBM supply is sold out.

Falling equities and rising prices for the scarcest component in the chain do not describe a
demand problem. They describe a **financing** problem — and that distinction is the single most
useful thing to carry into the week.

## What actually changed

The four largest US hyperscalers have guided to roughly **$725bn of capex in 2026, up about 77%**
on last year. Nobody disputes the spending. What changed in the last six weeks is that investors
started asking who absorbs it.

The answer is arriving in the cash flow statements:

| | Trailing free cash flow |
|---|---|
| **Amazon** | ~$26bn a year ago → **~$1.2bn** |
| **Alphabet** | **−38%** year-on-year |
| **Microsoft** | **−22%** year-on-year |

And the depreciation has barely started. The four hyperscalers bought about **$433.9bn** of
property and equipment in the four quarters to March 2026 while recognising roughly **$149bn**
of depreciation. That gap is not an accounting choice — servers run on 5–6 year schedules,
buildings on 25–40. The remainder lands on future income statements **whether or not the AI
revenue shows up.**

Roughly two-thirds of Microsoft's recent capex went into short-lived assets — GPUs and CPUs on
three-to-five year lives. This is not patient infrastructure. It is a rapidly-depreciating asset
base that needs continuous reinvestment to stand still.

Alphabet reported, beat, raised capex — and triggered a sector-wide selloff. **That is the tell.
For three years the market rewarded whoever spent most. It now rewards whoever can show a return,
or better, whoever does not need to spend at all.**

## How we are positioned into it

Our whole book is built on one distinction: **does this company pay, or does it get paid?**

- **Own the ones getting paid in cash, today, into a constrained order book.** NVDA at ~23x
  forward. TSM at 24.5x with 60% operating margins. MU at **5.3x forward** while its prices rise.
  AVGO at a 0.44 PEG.
- **Avoid the ones funding it.** AMZN, where consensus forward P/E now sits *above* trailing —
  the market explicitly modelling an EPS decline. ORCL, carrying $124.7bn of debt for
  counterparties whose ability to pay we cannot verify from public filings.
- **Avoid what is priced for perfection regardless of which side it sits on.** AMD at 66x
  forward with a 14% operating margin. ARM at 109x. PLTR at 56x sales *after* falling 41%.

We are Overweight 11 names, Neutral 8, Underweight 5. The full book, with targets and the
reasoning, is in [Coverage](../coverage/).

## The week ahead

**Tuesday 4 August — AMD reports.** Consensus around **$1.61 EPS on ~$11.3bn revenue (+47%)**.
We rate it Underweight, target $330. Watch the *margin* line, not the revenue line: at 66x
forward with a 14.4% operating margin, a revenue beat with flat gross margin is probably not
enough. This is a binary event and a genuine risk to our call in both directions.

**This week — Palantir and Arista report.** PLTR is our other Underweight into a print (target
$95, trading around $123). ANET is Neutral at 47x forward with a consensus target only ~7% above
the price — the street sees the ceiling too.

**Friday 7 August — US July non-farm payrolls, 8:30 ET.** A hot print revives the rate story on
top of an already-fragile tape. A weak print is genuinely ambiguous, because it cuts both rates
and growth.

## The one thing to watch

**Sequential DRAM contract prices.**

If they keep rising while memory equities fall, the divergence widens and our Micron and SK hynix
positions get more interesting, not less. If they turn negative, our memory thesis is not
weakened — it is **over**, and we will say so here.

We have written that trigger down in advance, in public, in [the Micron
note](../coverage/MU.html). Deciding what would change your mind *before* you need to is most of
what separates a process from an opinion.
