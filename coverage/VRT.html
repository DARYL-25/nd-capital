Our view

Investment thesis

Vertiv supplies the power distribution and thermal management systems that datacentres require, and rack power densities from modern AI systems have made cooling a genuine gating constraint on deployment rather than an afterthought. Revenue is growing 24.1% with a 20.4% operating margin and a 43.9% return on equity. The stock has fallen roughly 21% below its 50-day average in the broad AI selloff despite having none of the characteristics that selloff is about — Vertiv is a supplier paid on delivery, not a buyer financing a build-out with debt. At 37.6x forward it is not cheap, but the order book is tied to physical construction already committed, which gives better revenue visibility than the multiple implies.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

Thermal is a harder constraint than most models assume, and it is upstream of chip supply. Sector analysis is dominated by chip availability. But a rack that cannot be cooled cannot be deployed regardless of how many accelerators are available, and liquid cooling retrofits in existing facilities are slow, capital-intensive and skills-constrained. This makes cooling capacity a real bottleneck on deployment rates — which means Vertiv's revenue is gated by construction schedules already committed, not by next year's capex decisions.

Second, the selloff has not discriminated on business model. Vertiv fell with the AI complex despite being on the receiving end of the cash. This is exactly the mis-location of risk we identified for NVIDIA, in a smaller and less-followed name where the mispricing is more likely to persist.

Third — arguing against ourselves — Vertiv is a manufacturer, not a monopolist. Competition in power and cooling is real, margins are industrial rather than semiconductor-like, and a 37.6x multiple assumes the current pricing environment persists. That is the honest reason for Medium conviction.

Valuation conclusion

42x a forward EPS of about $6.43 gives $270, roughly 12% upside. Consensus is ~$339. We are 20% below because we apply a discount for competitive intensity that we think the market under-weights in a business with industrial rather than semiconductor economics. On EV/EBITDA at 36.5x this is not a cheap stock on any measure, and the case rests entirely on the physical bottleneck persisting.

Sector-specific read:

Orders and book-to-bill — the forward indicator that matters most in this business. Then: backlog, gross margin, liquid cooling revenue as a share of total, and the Americas revenue mix where AI datacentre construction is most concentrated.

Expectations & earnings reaction pattern

High earnings risk given the multiple and the beta of 2.03. What this stock needs to go up is orders, not earnings — in a capital-equipment business with a backlog, the order line is the forward-looking disclosure and the market reads it first.

Narrative read:

Roughly at the 200-day and 21% below the 50-day — a sharp correction from strength. Vertiv is a high-beta expression of the datacentre build-out and it has behaved like one. The correction is sector-driven rather than company-specific; nothing in the reported numbers has deteriorated.

Reminder: a stock with perfect fundamentals that the market has decided to dislike will not go up. Momentum is evidence, not noise.

Management & guidance behaviour

Realistic communication with a credible operational turnaround record — margins have improved substantially over several years, which is delivered rather than promised. Capital allocation has been disciplined. Guidance tracker not built; flagged to B2.

Teaching note

The lesson is look for the constraint that is not the obvious one. Everyone models chip supply. Far fewer model whether the buildings can power and cool the chips. Constraints move around a supply chain over time, and the binding one is frequently the least glamorous — in this cycle it has been advanced packaging, then HBM, and increasingly power and thermal.

The practical exercise: for any theme, list every physical input required to deliver the end product, then rank them by lead time. The longest lead time is usually the real constraint, and the company that owns it has pricing power that no comps table will show you — because the comps table compares it to other electrical equipment companies, not to the AI complex whose growth it actually gates. Mis-categorised companies are one of the most reliable sources of mispricing, because screens sort by sector code and sector codes do not know what a company has become.
