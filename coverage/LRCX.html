Our view

Investment thesis

Lam Research supplies the etch and deposition tools that memory fabs depend on most heavily, which makes it the purest equipment expression of the memory cycle. With DRAM contract prices rising sharply and all three producers shifting capacity toward HBM and server DRAM, the capex that follows lands disproportionately in Lam's order book. Revenue is growing 30% year-on-year with a 65% return on equity and 37% operating margins. At 32.1x forward it is priced similarly to peers with less memory leverage, which we think under-prices the specific cyclical positioning. The risk is symmetrical and should not be understated: the same leverage that works now works violently in reverse when memory capex turns.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

Memory leverage is an asset right now and it is being priced as a generic semicap risk. Lam, Applied Materials and KLA trade in a narrow band of forward multiples (32–34x) despite very different exposure to the cycle that is actually accelerating. Applied Materials is growing revenue at 11.4%; Lam at 30%. Similar multiples on nearly triple the growth rate is the specific mispricing we are underwriting — and the comparison is more reliable than a comparison across sectors because these three businesses face the same customers and the same cycle.

Second, HBM manufacturing is process-step intensive in ways that favour etch and deposition. Stacking and through-silicon-via formation add steps relative to conventional DRAM, which means HBM's share gain increases tool intensity per wafer, not just wafer count. That is a structural content-per-wafer gain layered on top of a volume cycle.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Sector-specific read:

Memory as a percentage of systems revenue — the leverage measure. Then: deferred revenue and backlog, installed-base (spares and service) revenue as the cyclical shock absorber, China revenue share, and gross margin.

Expectations & earnings reaction pattern

Semicap earnings are backward-looking relative to the cycle: tools ship on orders placed months earlier. What this stock needs to go up is not a beat but continued evidence of memory capex intent — competitor capex announcements move it more than its own results do, which is unusual and worth knowing.

Management & guidance behaviour

Realistic guidance culture with a good record of managing through memory down-cycles — a capability that matters more in this business than in most, because the cycle amplitude is extreme. Capital returns have been consistent. Guidance tracker not built; flagged to B2.

Teaching note

The lesson is use tight comparables to isolate a single variable. Comparing Lam to the market, or even to semiconductors broadly, tells you almost nothing — too many differences. But comparing Lam, Applied Materials and KLA is close to a controlled experiment: same customers, same cycle, same geography, same policy exposure. When three near-identical businesses trade at similar multiples on very different growth rates, the mispricing is unusually clean because you have eliminated most of the alternative explanations. Build your comp sets to be as narrow as possible; a comp table with twenty names in it is usually a comp table that has explained nothing.
