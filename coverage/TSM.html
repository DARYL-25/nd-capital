Our view

Investment thesis

TSMC is the one genuinely non-substitutable asset in the AI supply chain: NVIDIA's GPUs, Broadcom's and Google's custom silicon, AMD's accelerators and Apple's processors are all manufactured here, which means the architecture debate that dominates sector commentary is almost entirely irrelevant to TSMC's revenue. Whoever wins, TSMC fabricates it. Revenue is growing 36% year-on-year at a 60% operating margin and a ~64% gross margin — margins that are extraordinary for a capital-intensive manufacturer and that reflect genuine pricing power at the leading edge. The constraint on the entire industry is advanced packaging capacity (CoWoS), which TSMC owns and allocates. At 24.5x forward earnings we are paying a moderate multiple for the closest thing to a toll booth in technology. The risk that justifies any discount at all is geographic, not competitive, and it is not a risk we can hedge or forecast — only size for.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

The market prices TSMC as a semiconductor cyclical and it has stopped behaving like one. Foundry economics at the leading edge have consolidated to a near-monopoly: the customer has nowhere else to take a 3nm or 2nm design at volume. That has changed the margin profile structurally — 60% operating margins are not cyclical peak behaviour, they are the arithmetic of having one supplier. The multiple still embeds a cyclicality assumption that the industry structure no longer supports.

Second, packaging capacity is the real bottleneck and it is under-analysed. Sector commentary focuses relentlessly on wafer nodes. But through this cycle, the gating factor on how many accelerators reach a rack has repeatedly been CoWoS packaging and HBM supply, not lithography. TSMC controls the first and allocates against the second. Capacity allocation decisions made here determine who ships — which is a form of pricing power that never appears in a comps table.

Third, the geopolitical discount is applied continuously and priced as if the risk were constant. It is not constant; it moves with policy. But because it is uncomfortable to model, most valuation work applies a static haircut and stops thinking. We do the same — honestly, we have no edge on Taiwan Strait risk — but we flag that the discount is a placeholder for un-modelled risk, not a measured one.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Specific and monitorable. If any of these prints, we change the view in public.

Specific and monitorable. Agent A3 (Thesis Guardian) watches these. Vague conditions are useless.

  1. Monthly revenue growth decelerates below 15% year-on-year for two consecutive months. TSMC reports monthly, which gives us an unusually high-frequency disconfirming signal — use it.
  2. Gross margin guidance falls below 55%, indicating either pricing pressure or a mix shift away from leading edge.
  3. Advanced packaging capacity expansion outpaces bookings, turning the bottleneck into overcapacity — visible in capex guidance rising while lead times shorten.

Valuation conclusion

24.5x forward for 36% revenue growth and 60% operating margins is not demanding. We apply 28x to a forward EPS of roughly $16.50 for a $460 target, ~14% upside. Consensus is at ~$540; we are below, principally because we apply a geopolitical discount that we cannot defend precisely and would rather state openly than bury. Sector-specific read:

Monthly revenue (uniquely high-frequency for a company of this size — treat it as the single best real-time indicator of AI demand available in public markets), advanced-node revenue mix (3nm and below as a share of wafer revenue), gross margin, CoWoS capacity and utilisation, and capex guidance as the forward-demand signal.

Expectations & earnings reaction pattern

Because TSMC reports revenue monthly, expectations reset twelve times a year rather than four. That structurally reduces earnings-event risk and is why we carry Medium rather than High earnings risk despite the name's beta to the AI trade. What this stock needs to go up is straightforward and unusually observable: monthly revenue growth sustaining above 25%.

Reminder: a stock with perfect fundamentals that the market has decided to dislike will not go up. Momentum is evidence, not noise.

Management & guidance behaviour

Guidance tracker not yet built. Observable pattern: TSMC guides conservatively on margin and raises capex when demand justifies it, which is the behaviour of a management team that expects to be held to its numbers. Disclosure quality is high by sector standards — monthly revenue in particular is a governance strength that materially improves the analysability of the name.

Teaching note

The lesson is find the bottleneck, not the brand. In any supply chain under strain, the economics accrue to whoever controls the scarcest step — and that step is often unglamorous and several layers away from the company whose logo is on the product. Here the scarce step has been advanced packaging, not chip design. A practical habit: for any theme you cover, write down the full physical sequence from raw input to delivered product, then ask which step has the longest lead time and the fewest qualified suppliers. That is where pricing power sits, and it is rarely where the headlines are. Note also the value of reporting frequency as an analytical asset — a company that reports monthly is a fundamentally more knowable company, and knowability deserves a premium.
