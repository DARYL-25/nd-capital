Our view

Investment thesis

Salesforce trades at 13.5x forward earnings — a multiple normally associated with declining industrials, not with a software franchise growing revenue 13.3% at a 78% gross margin with EPS up 52% year-on-year. The de-rating reflects a genuine and serious fear: that agentic AI collapses the seat-based licensing model by reducing the number of humans who need a seat. We take that risk seriously and it is the reason conviction is Medium. But the evidence has not yet appeared in the numbers — revenue growth is stable and margins are expanding — and 13.5x is a multiple that requires the disruption to be well underway to be justified. We are being paid to take a risk that has been priced as though it were already a fact.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

The market is pricing the agentic-AI disruption as a certainty and pricing Salesforce's own agentic product as worthless. Both cannot be true simultaneously. If agents genuinely restructure enterprise software workflows, the incumbent with the customer relationships, the data, and the compliance and integration footprint has a credible path to selling agent capacity instead of seats. Salesforce might lose that transition — plenty of incumbents do — but at 13.5x forward the market is charging nothing for the possibility that it wins.

Second, the seat-count fear may have the direction wrong. The bear case assumes agents replace users, reducing seats. The alternative is that agents become billable units — consumption pricing layered on top of, or replacing, seats. That would be a revenue model change rather than a revenue decline, and revenue model changes are exactly what markets misprice, because the transition is messy and the terminal state is uncertain.

Third, EPS grew 52% while the stock de-rated. That divergence between delivered earnings and multiple is the mechanical core of the opportunity.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Specific and monitorable. If any of these prints, we change the view in public.

Specific and monitorable. Agent A3 (Thesis Guardian) watches these. Vague conditions are useless.

  1. Subscription revenue growth falls below 8% for two consecutive quarters — the first hard evidence that seat compression is real rather than feared.
  2. Net revenue retention declines materially, which would show existing customers shrinking their commitments rather than expanding them. This is the single best early indicator.
  3. Operating margin expansion stalls while growth decelerates, indicating the company is spending to defend a shrinking base.

Valuation conclusion

17x a forward EPS of about $13.62 gives $225, roughly 22% upside. Consensus is ~$242. We are below consensus and Overweight — again deliberately. The valuation case does not need multiple expansion beyond 17x, which is still a discount to the broad market for a business with 78% gross margins. If the disruption thesis is wrong, this is worth considerably more than $225; we are simply not underwriting that in the base case.

Sector-specific read:

Net revenue retention — the cleanest early read on whether seats are compressing. Then: subscription revenue growth, current remaining performance obligation, operating margin, and any disclosed agent consumption or pricing metric.

Expectations & earnings reaction pattern

Expectations are low, which is the point. A stock at 13.5x forward does not need to beat; it needs to demonstrate that the disruption is not happening. Stable net revenue retention would likely do more for this stock than an EPS beat. The bar has moved from growth to survival, and that is a much easier bar to clear.

Teaching note

The lesson is distinguishing a priced risk from an unpriced one. Everyone in the market knows about the agentic-AI threat to seat-based software; it is discussed constantly. That is precisely why it is unlikely to be a source of return on the short side — consensus risks are, almost by definition, in the price. The question is never "is this risk real?" but "is this risk more or less severe than the price implies?" At 13.5x forward with earnings growing 52%, the price implies the disruption is already happening. It is not, yet. That gap between the priced scenario and the observed reality is the entire trade.

The corollary is a habit worth building: for any widely-discussed risk, work out what multiple the stock would trade at if the risk were certain, and compare it to the actual multiple. If they are the same, you are being paid to take the other side, and you only need the consensus to be early rather than wrong.
