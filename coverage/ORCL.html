Our view

Investment thesis

Oracle is the most interesting and least resolvable name in our coverage. The business is performing: revenue growing 20.6%, a remaining-performance-obligation backlog reported up roughly 325% to around $638bn, and AI infrastructure gross margins above 30% alongside a multi-cloud database business at 60–80%. The equity has fallen more than 50% from its high and around 47% since the start of June. The reason is financing and counterparty risk: Oracle carries approximately $124.7bn of debt, raised $43bn of debt and $5bn of equity in the fiscal year to May 2026, plans roughly $40bn more in FY27, and disclosed in its own annual report that some customers "may be highly leveraged" with attendant non-payment risk. The honest conclusion is that the outcome depends on a fact we cannot obtain from public information — whether Oracle's OCI counterparties can pay. We rate Neutral with Low conviction and publish the distribution rather than pretend to a point estimate.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

The market is pricing a credit outcome using equity tools, and doing it crudely. Oracle's valuation now hinges on counterparty credit quality — a question that credit analysts are equipped for and equity screens are not. A backlog of $638bn is an asset if the counterparties pay and a liability-generating machine if they do not, because Oracle has already borrowed to build the capacity to serve it. Almost nothing about that question is visible in a P/E ratio.

Second, the company disclosed the risk itself. The annual report language about highly leveraged customers and non-payment risk is unusually direct. Managements do not volunteer that kind of disclosure lightly, and we read it as a genuine signal rather than boilerplate — which is a point for the bears, not against them.

Third, this is the visible edge of the emerging theme we flagged in the house view: the financing layer of AI. If AI infrastructure debt becomes a distinct credit asset class, Oracle will be its benchmark issuer, and its equity will trade on funding spreads rather than on software multiples.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Specific and monitorable. If any of these prints, we change the view in public.

Specific and monitorable. Agent A3 (Thesis Guardian) watches these. Vague conditions are useless.

  1. Any OCI counterparty publicly restructures, defaults, or renegotiates a major commitment. This is the bear trigger and it would be immediate and severe.
  2. Oracle raises the FY27 debt plan above $40bn, or issues equity at depressed prices — either would confirm the funding structure is under strain.
  3. RPO backlog converts to revenue at or above the contracted schedule for two consecutive quarters, which would substantially validate the bull case and should trigger an upgrade.

Valuation conclusion

17x a forward EPS of about $8.10 gives $140, roughly 8% above the indicative price. Consensus is ~$248, which is a very wide gap to our number and reflects the street's willingness to underwrite backlog conversion at close to face value. We are not willing to do that without visibility into counterparty credit. On a static basis the stock looks inexpensive — 16.1x forward for 20.6% revenue growth — and that cheapness is entirely a function of an unpriced credit question.

Sector-specific read:

Remaining performance obligation and its conversion rate into recognised revenue — the backlog number alone is nearly meaningless without conversion. Then: total debt and net leverage, interest expense, OCI gross margin trajectory, customer concentration disclosure, and free cash flow after capex. For this name specifically, the corporate bond spread is a more informative daily signal than the share price.

Expectations & earnings reaction pattern

Very high uncertainty in both directions. The stock has fallen far enough that modest good news could produce a violent rally, and the leverage means modest bad news could do the reverse. This is the one name in our coverage where we would explicitly say position sizing matters more than the target price, and where an options structure would express the view better than the equity.

Management & guidance behaviour

Promotional communication style with a long record of framing cloud positioning ambitiously. The disclosure on customer leverage risk is a notable and creditable exception. The central question is whether the pace of debt-funded capacity commitment reflects justified conviction in contracted demand or an underwriting standard that has loosened. We cannot determine this from public information, and saying so is the finding. Guidance tracker not built; flagged to B2.

Alternative data & sentiment

Not instrumented. For this name the most valuable alternative data is not social sentiment but credit market data — Oracle bond spreads and CDS levels, which price the exact risk the equity market is struggling with. Flagged to C2 as a distinct and high-priority build.

Teaching note

Oracle is the most instructive name in this initiation, for a reason that has nothing to do with software.

When a company's equity value depends on whether someone else can pay, you are doing credit analysis whether you realise it or not. Equity analysts are trained to model revenue, margin and multiple. None of those tools answer "will the customer pay?" — and when that becomes the binding question, the usual toolkit produces confident-looking answers to the wrong question. The correct response is to change tools: look at the bond spread, the debt maturity schedule, the customer's own balance sheet.

Second: a bimodal distribution should not be collapsed into a point estimate. We produced a $146 probability-weighted value and then told you it is close to meaningless, because the real outcomes are approximately $70 or approximately $260. Averaging two futures that will never both happen produces a number describing a world that does not exist. When you find yourself in that situation, publish the scenarios, size the position for the bear case, and resist the pressure to produce a single number just because the format expects one.

Third, and hardest: "we cannot know this from public information" is a legitimate research output. It is more useful than a fabricated view, because it tells the reader exactly what to go and find out.
