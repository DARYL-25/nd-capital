Our view

Investment thesis

Arista builds the best datacentre switching systems available and has executed superbly through the AI build-out, with revenue growing 35.1% at a 42.7% operating margin. The problem is that it trades at 47.2x forward earnings — the second-highest multiple in our coverage outside the software names — while facing structural pressure from merchant silicon. Broadcom supplies the switching chips that enable white-box alternatives, and hyperscalers are precisely the customers most capable of, and most motivated by, building their own systems around them. We are Overweight Broadcom and Neutral Arista on the same underlying observation. The consensus target sits only about 7% above the current price, which is unusually tight and tells us the street sees limited room too.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

Customer concentration and customer capability are the same risk here, and that is unusual. Arista's largest customers are hyperscalers — the small set of companies with the engineering depth to design their own switching around merchant silicon and the volume to justify it. In most businesses, concentration risk means a customer might leave for a competitor. Here, the customer is the competitor in waiting. That deserves a valuation discount rather than a growth premium, and at 47x forward it is not receiving one.

Second, the market is not distinguishing between AI networking demand and Arista's share of it. Networking demand from AI clusters is unambiguously growing. Arista's share of that demand is the actual question, and it is a different question — one that merchant-silicon economics answer unfavourably over time.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Valuation conclusion

45x a forward EPS of about $3.80 gives $180 — essentially flat. Consensus is ~$192, only 7% above the price, which is one of the tightest consensus spreads in our coverage and indicates the street shares our sense of limited headroom. We would become interested below roughly $140, where the multiple would compensate for the merchant-silicon risk.

Teaching note

The lesson is read customer concentration for capability, not just for size. Standard risk analysis flags concentration when a few customers make up a large share of revenue. The sharper question is whether those specific customers could do this themselves. A concentrated customer base of small enterprises is a very different risk from a concentrated base of the four most technically sophisticated infrastructure companies on earth. In the second case, your customer is your most credible potential competitor, and the relationship caps your pricing power permanently, not just during negotiations.

Note too the pairing: Overweight AVGO / Neutral ANET expresses one view — merchant silicon takes share from integrated systems — through two positions. Expressing a single insight through a relative pair is usually more robust than betting the whole view on one leg, because the shared sector risk cancels.
