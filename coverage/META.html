Our view

Investment thesis

Meta presents the cleanest illustration of the AI capex problem: revenue grew 28% year-on-year while EPS fell 13%. Growth is being converted into margin compression by a capex programme raised to $125–145bn for 2026. The stock has fallen roughly 30% from its high and sits below its 200-day average, so a good deal of this is priced. At 18.6x forward earnings Meta is not expensive for the quality of the advertising franchise. But we cannot underwrite the earnings trajectory while capex is still being raised and the return on it remains undisclosed, and we are not willing to call a bottom on a name where management has a documented history of very large, very persistent spending on projects with no measurable return. Neutral, low conviction, and we would rather be honest about the low conviction than manufacture a view.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

We do not claim an information edge here. The observation we would make is structural: Meta is the only hyperscale spender whose AI capex has no external customer. Microsoft, Amazon, Google and Oracle sell cloud capacity — their build-out has a revenue line attached, however imperfectly. Meta's build-out serves its own advertising ranking and its own model development. That means the return is real but never directly observable, and unobservable returns are priced conservatively by markets, correctly so.

The corollary is that Meta's capex can never be validated by a revenue disclosure the way AWS or Azure capacity can. Investors are being asked to take the return on roughly $135bn on faith, from a management team that spent years and tens of billions on Reality Labs. That is not a scandal; it is a structural reason why this name should trade at a discount to peers on the same growth — and it is not obvious the discount is yet large enough.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Specific and monitorable. If any of these prints, we change the view in public.

  1. EPS returns to year-on-year growth while capex stays elevated — the direct refutation of the margin-compression concern.
  2. Capex guidance is lowered or held flat for 2027, indicating discipline rather than escalation.
  3. Management discloses a measurable return metric on AI spend (revenue per user attributable to ranking improvements, or similar). Disclosure alone would justify a re-rating.

Valuation conclusion

20x a forward EPS of about $29.90 gives $595, roughly 7% upside — not enough to be interesting given the earnings uncertainty. Consensus is at ~$769, implying about 26x, which we think is too generous for a business with declining EPS and unvalidatable capex. We are 23% below consensus and rated Neutral. The valuation looks superficially attractive on almost every static multiple; the problem is entirely in the direction of the earnings.

Sector-specific read:

Advertising revenue growth and, more importantly, revenue per user — the latter is the only place where AI ranking improvements can show up as evidence. Then: total capex and its year-on-year change, operating margin ex-Reality Labs, Reality Labs operating loss, and depreciation as a share of revenue.

Expectations & earnings reaction pattern

The market is currently rewarding capital discipline over growth across the sector. Meta is on the wrong side of that preference, having raised capex into a de-rating. This stock needs a capex plateau far more than it needs a revenue beat — and a beat accompanied by another capex raise would probably see the shares fall, which is the pattern already established across the group this quarter.

Narrative read:

Below both moving averages and roughly 30% off the high — the weakest trend structure among the megacaps. Our principles are explicit that the market's decided narrative tends to win over 6–12 months, and the narrative here has turned. That is a genuine reason not to be constructive on a valuation argument alone, and it is factored into the Low conviction.

Reminder: a stock with perfect fundamentals that the market has decided to dislike will not go up. Momentum is evidence, not noise.

Management & guidance behaviour

The relevant history is Reality Labs: sustained, very large spending against a vision, with losses that persisted well past the point where most managements would have retrenched. Read generously that is conviction; read sceptically it is a governance structure with limited external check, given the dual-class share arrangement. Both readings are defensible and an investor's view of this stock is largely determined by which one they hold. We do not have a strong view on which is correct, and that is the honest reason conviction is Low.

Alternative data & sentiment

Not instrumented, and this is a name where alt-data would genuinely help — app engagement and ad load trends are measurable in ways that the AI capex return is not. Flagged to C2 as a priority-two build.

Standing caveat: social and alternative data is noisy, reflexive, and frequently a contrarian indicator at extremes. Where we have not measured something, we say so rather than inferring it.

Teaching note

The lesson here is about the difference between a cheap multiple and a cheap stock. Meta at 18.6x forward looks inexpensive against a 28%-growing revenue base. But the multiple is applied to an earnings number that is falling, and a low multiple on a declining E is not value — it is a value trap in formation. The habit to build: before reacting to any multiple, look at the first derivative of the denominator. Is E rising or falling? A 20x multiple on rising earnings and a 20x multiple on falling earnings are completely different investments that look identical on a screen. The second lesson is subtler: be suspicious of capex that can never be validated by a disclosure. When spending serves an internal purpose only, no future disclosure can ever prove it worked — which means the discount the market applies will never be removed by evidence. That is a permanent structural discount, not a temporary sentiment one.
