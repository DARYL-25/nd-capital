Our view

Investment thesis

KLA dominates process control — the inspection and metrology systems that find defects during manufacturing. Its economic position improves structurally as chips get harder to make: smaller geometries and complex 3D packaging both raise the cost of a defect and therefore the value of finding it early. An 87.5% return on equity and 42.5% operating margins reflect a near-monopoly in the most defensible niche of semicap. Revenue growth of 15.2% is slower than Lam's, but the business is less cyclically levered and the margin structure is superior. At 33.8x forward it is priced in line with peers despite better returns on capital, which we think is the mispricing.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

Advanced packaging is a process-control problem and that is under-modelled. As the industry moves value from lithography to packaging — chiplets, stacked die, through-silicon vias — the number of inspection steps rises disproportionately, because a defect discovered after stacking destroys multiple good die rather than one. The economic case for inspection strengthens exactly as packaging complexity increases. KLA's addressable content per wafer is therefore rising structurally, independent of wafer volumes.

Second, the returns profile is not reflected in the relative multiple. KLA earns an 87.5% return on equity against Lam's 65% and Applied's 40%, yet all three trade at 32–34x forward. In a narrow comp set where the businesses face identical demand, a persistent returns advantage should command a multiple premium. It does not.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Teaching note

The lesson is returns on capital as a multiple determinant within a comp set. Two companies with the same growth rate but different returns on invested capital should not trade at the same multiple — the higher-return business converts each unit of growth into more shareholder value and requires less reinvestment to do it. When you find near-identical businesses at identical multiples with materially different ROE or ROIC, you have found either a mispricing or an un-modelled risk. Your job is to work out which, and the burden of proof should sit with finding the risk. Here we could not find one, so we took the mispricing — while noting that "I could not find the reason" is a weaker statement than "there is no reason."

Note also the practical value of the ranking over the targets. Point targets carry false precision; a defensible ordering within a tight comp set is more robust and more actionable.
