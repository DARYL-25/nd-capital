Our view

Investment thesis

AMD trades at 66x forward earnings with a 14.4% operating margin and an 8% return on equity, while NVIDIA — a better business by every operating measure — trades at 23x. That relative valuation is the entire call. The stock has roughly tripled from its 52-week low and sits about 53% above its 200-day average, so this is a crowded, extended position in a market that has just turned hostile to exactly this profile. AMD's data-centre execution has genuinely improved and we are not disputing the business trajectory; we are disputing the price of it. Reports Tuesday 4 August with consensus near $1.61 EPS on roughly $11.3bn of revenue (+47%) — a binary event that could easily invalidate the timing of this call in either direction, and position sizing should reflect that rather than the target price.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

The relative-value gap inside merchant GPU is the widest it has been in this cycle and it is being ignored because both names are labelled "AI." An investor who owns AMD instead of NVIDIA is paying nearly three times the forward multiple for a fraction of the margin and a weaker software moat. In a de-rating, indiscriminate theme exposure gets repriced first and the lower-quality expression falls furthest. That is not a prediction about AMD's products; it is an observation about how dispersion resolves.

Second, the operating margin is the tell. At 14.4%, AMD is not capturing accelerator economics the way a genuine architectural winner would. High-margin AI silicon revenue should pull blended margins up sharply. It has not, which suggests the AI revenue mix is smaller, or priced more competitively, than the narrative implies.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Specific and monitorable. If any of these prints, we change the view in public.

  3. Operating margin exceeds 25%, which would show the mix shift is real and our margin-based scepticism is wrong.

Valuation conclusion

45x a forward EPS of about $7.20 gives $330, roughly 31% downside. Consensus sits at ~$579, which implies about 80x forward — a multiple we cannot construct a defensible bridge to. We are 43% below consensus, the widest gap in our coverage, and that gap should itself be treated as a warning: when a view is this far from the street, the base rate favours the street. EV/EBITDA of 95x is the highest in our universe by a wide margin.

Sector-specific read:

Data-centre revenue growth and, critically, data-centre gross margin — growth without margin means AMD is buying share. Then: blended operating margin, MI-series revenue disclosure, client and gaming segment trends, and inventory days.

Expectations & earnings reaction pattern

Very high earnings risk. The stock has tripled, sits at 66x forward, and reports in two days. Our standards ask what a stock needs to do to go up: AMD needs a margin surprise, not a revenue beat. Revenue beats are largely expected at +47% consensus growth. If revenue beats and gross margin is flat, we would expect the shares to fall — the same pattern the megacaps have already demonstrated this quarter.

Momentum is strongly positive and our own principles are explicit that positive momentum should not be dismissed — a stock the market has decided to like can stay expensive for a long time. We are deliberately fighting the trend here, which is the highest-risk posture in our framework. The justification is the extremity of the relative valuation, not any technical signal. If we are wrong on this name, it will be because we underestimated momentum, and we are recording that in advance.

Reminder: a stock with perfect fundamentals that the market has decided to dislike will not go up. Momentum is evidence, not noise.

Management & guidance behaviour

AMD's management communicates ambitiously and has a pattern of framing total addressable market opportunity prominently in guidance. That is a promotional profile, and it warrants a discount to guided numbers rather than a premium. The execution record on CPU share against Intel is genuinely excellent and should not be dismissed — the concern is specific to how AI revenue expectations are set, not to operational capability. Teaching note

The core discipline here is relative valuation within a theme. When a sector runs, correlation rises and quality distinctions get erased — everything labelled "AI" trades together. When the theme de-rates, those distinctions reassert violently, and the lowest-quality expression of a theme falls furthest. So the productive question is rarely "is this sector cheap?" but "within this sector, what am I paying per unit of margin and returns on capital?"

Note also two pieces of intellectual honesty embedded above that are worth copying. We flagged that our target is 43% below consensus and said explicitly that such a gap is itself evidence we might be wrong. And we recorded in advance the reason we would most likely be wrong (momentum). Writing down your expected failure mode before the outcome is known is the only way to later distinguish a bad process from bad luck.
