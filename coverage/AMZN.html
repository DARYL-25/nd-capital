Our view

Investment thesis

Amazon's forward P/E of 32.1x sits above its trailing P/E of 21.8x, which is consensus stating plainly that it expects earnings per share to fall. That is unusual for a company growing revenue at 19.6%, and it is the clearest single piece of evidence in our coverage that the depreciation wave is real and arriving. Trailing free cash flow has collapsed from roughly $26bn a year ago to about $1.2bn, while 2026 capex is guided to around $200bn — the largest in the group. AWS is a genuinely excellent business and Trainium is a real cost lever, but at 32x forward earnings on declining free cash flow we think the risk is priced the wrong way round. We would rather own the companies receiving this $200bn than the company spending it.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

The market has not fully reconciled the free-cash-flow collapse with the multiple. Amazon has historically been valued on cash flow because its earnings understate its economics. That argument runs in reverse right now: cash flow has fallen 95% while the equity trades near a premium multiple on earnings. When the traditional valuation anchor for a business becomes inconvenient and the market quietly switches anchors, that is worth noticing.

Second, the inverted P/E structure is under-discussed. Forward above trailing is a rare and informative configuration. Most screens and most commentary quote one or the other, so the inversion passes without comment. It is a compact summary of the entire bear case.

Third — arguing against ourselves — Trainium is a genuine offset. Like Alphabet's TPU, in-house silicon means part of the capex stays inside the company rather than paying a supplier's margin. That is the strongest bull argument and it is why this is Medium rather than high conviction. If Trainium scales faster than we expect, the unit economics of AWS AI improve materially and our call is wrong.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Specific and monitorable. If any of these prints, we change the view in public.

Specific and monitorable. Agent A3 (Thesis Guardian) watches these. Vague conditions are useless.

  1. Free cash flow inflects positively for two consecutive quarters while capex remains at guided levels. That would break the core of our argument.
  2. AWS operating margin expands through the depreciation ramp rather than compressing.
  3. Trainium is disclosed as a materially larger share of AWS AI capacity, confirming the in-house cost advantage is scaling.

Valuation conclusion

28x a forward EPS of about $8.50 gives $235, roughly 13% downside. Consensus sits at ~$322, which requires either the EPS decline not to happen or a 38x multiple on the depressed number. We are 27% below consensus. On EV/EBITDA the name looks reasonable at 11.9x, which is the strongest counter to our view — EBITDA excludes exactly the depreciation we are worried about, so the choice of metric here is not neutral. An investor who thinks in EV/EBITDA will disagree with this rating, and the disagreement is entirely about whether depreciation is a real cost. We think it is.

Sector-specific read:

Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow — this is the number the thesis lives or dies on. Then: AWS revenue growth and AWS operating margin together, capex versus operating cash flow, depreciation as a share of revenue, and any disclosure on Trainium capacity share.

Expectations & earnings reaction pattern

Because consensus already models an EPS decline, the bar on earnings is genuinely low — this is not a stock that needs to beat. It needs free cash flow to stop deteriorating. Investors should watch the cash flow statement first and the income statement second, which is the reverse of the usual reading order and is the single most practical piece of advice we can give on this name.

Management & guidance behaviour

Operationally credible with a long record of investing through scepticism and being vindicated, which is the strongest argument against our Underweight and we take it seriously. The counter-argument is that the previous investment cycles (fulfilment, AWS itself) built assets with 10–25 year lives, whereas GPUs depreciate in three to five. The same management playbook applied to a shorter-lived asset produces a very different cash-flow profile, and we do not think the market has adjusted its Amazon heuristic for that change. Disclosure on AI unit economics is thin.

Teaching note

Two durable lessons.

First, when forward P/E exceeds trailing P/E, stop and find out why. It means the market expects earnings to fall. It is one of the most information-dense single comparisons available and it takes five seconds. Most people quote one multiple and never look at the pair.

Second, notice when the customary valuation metric for a company quietly changes. Amazon was valued on cash flow for two decades because earnings understated it. Now that cash flow has collapsed, the conversation has migrated to earnings and EBITDA. Whenever a company's preferred metric changes at the same time as its numbers deteriorate, be suspicious — of the metric, not necessarily the company. EBITDA in particular is precisely the wrong lens for a business whose central issue is the depreciation of short-lived assets. Choosing the metric is most of the analysis, and it is usually done unconsciously.
