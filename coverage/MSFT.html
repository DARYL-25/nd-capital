Our view

Investment thesis

Microsoft is the highest-quality asset in our coverage and we cannot make a case for owning it over the alternatives at 23.6x forward earnings. Revenue is growing 17.7% with 45% operating margins and the Azure/Copilot position is genuinely strong. But roughly two-thirds of this quarter's capex went into short-lived assets — GPUs and CPUs on three-to-five year schedules — and free cash flow is already down 22% year-on-year. The depreciation from a $120bn-plus capex year does not depend on whether AI revenue materialises; it lands on the income statement on a fixed schedule. At 23.6x forward we are paying a full multiple for an earnings stream that has a known headwind building inside it. Neutral here is a real conclusion, not a hedge: the analysis did not produce a differentiated view, and our standards say that is a valid and valuable answer rather than something to disguise with a target.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

Candidly, we do not think we have identified something the market is missing on Microsoft. It is the most-covered equity in the world, the depreciation issue is well documented, and the Azure growth debate is thoroughly worked over by people with better channel checks than we have.

The one observation we would offer is a framing point rather than an information edge: forward P/E flatters companies in the early phase of a depreciation ramp. Consensus forward EPS typically under-models the D&A step-up because it anchors on management's margin guidance, and management guides margin before the full schedule lands. If that is right, Microsoft's true forward multiple is somewhat higher than 23.6x. That is a reason for caution rather than a thesis, and we present it as such.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Valuation conclusion

24x a forward EPS of about $19.70 gives $470 — roughly flat. Consensus is at ~$563, implying about 29x, which we think under-weights the depreciation ramp. We are 17% below consensus with a Neutral rating. We would become interested below roughly $400, where the multiple would compensate for the margin headwind; we would become cautious above $520.

Sector-specific read:

Azure growth with segment margin — growth alone is uninformative at this capex level. Depreciation as a percentage of revenue, tracked sequentially, is the single most important line in the model. Then: capex-to-operating-cash-flow, commercial bookings and remaining performance obligation, and any Copilot seat or ARPU disclosure.

Expectations & earnings reaction pattern

Microsoft currently needs to demonstrate capital discipline rather than growth, in line with the sector-wide shift in what the market is rewarding. Alphabet beat on earnings and fell on spending; Microsoft faces the same test. The bar for the stock is a capex guide that moderates, not an EPS beat.

Management & guidance behaviour

High-quality, consistent, and unusually good at managing the framing of a transition. That is a compliment and a caution: a management team skilled at narrative deserves more scrutiny of the numbers, not less. Watch specifically for any extension of server useful-life assumptions — that is the standard lever for protecting EPS during a depreciation ramp, it is entirely legal, and it is a quality-of-earnings red flag when it appears. We are not alleging it; we are saying it belongs on the checklist.

Teaching note

The teaching point is about the discipline of publishing a Neutral. There is enormous professional pressure to have a view on the largest companies, and the easy path is to manufacture a small edge and dress it as conviction. But a Neutral with clear reasoning and explicit levels — interested below $400, cautious above $520 — is more useful to a decision-maker than a fabricated Overweight, because it tells them what would need to change. The second point is mechanical and worth internalising: when a company is early in a capex-driven depreciation ramp, treat consensus forward EPS with suspicion. Estimates anchor on guided margins, and guided margins are set before the full schedule arrives. The forward P/E you see on a screen is probably optimistic.
