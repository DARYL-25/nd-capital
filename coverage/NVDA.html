Our view

Investment thesis

NVIDIA now trades at roughly 23x forward earnings while growing revenue 85% year-on-year at a 66% operating margin — a combination that has essentially never been available in a megacap. The de-rating since June has not been driven by any deterioration in NVIDIA's own results; it has been driven by the market's growing doubt about whether its customers can keep financing their purchases. That distinction matters enormously, because NVIDIA sits on the receiving end of the cash: it is paid up front, carries no build-out debt, and converts at extraordinary rates, whereas its customers are the ones issuing bonds and absorbing a depreciation wave. We think the market is applying customer-balance-sheet risk to the one company in the chain that does not carry it. The bear case is real and we hold it openly — if 2027 hyperscaler capex is cut, NVIDIA's growth rate halves — but at 23x forward we are being paid to take that risk rather than paying up to avoid it.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

Three things.

First, the market is confusing where the risk sits. The AI capex debate is a debate about balance sheets — Amazon's free cash flow falling from ~$26bn to ~$1.2bn, Oracle's $124.7bn of debt, Alphabet's FCF down 38%. NVIDIA has the opposite profile. It is the creditor of this cycle, not the debtor. Selling NVIDIA because hyperscaler financing looks stretched is like selling the bank because the borrower looks stretched — defensible only if you think the loan does not get repaid, which for already-committed 2026 orders it largely already has been.

Second, the forward multiple has quietly collapsed while the narrative stayed the same. NVIDIA at 23x forward is priced closer to a mature semiconductor company than to the asset the market spent two years describing as the most important company in the world. Either the growth is about to stop, or the multiple is wrong. The order book says the growth has not stopped yet.

Third, the de-rating is happening while dispersion inside AI is widening sharply. AMD trades at 66x forward and ARM at 109x; NVIDIA trades at 23x with better margins, better returns on capital and a far stronger competitive position. Within a theme that is selling off indiscriminately, relative value inside the theme is where the return is — and NVIDIA is now the cheap end of its own sector, which is not a sentence that has been true since 2023.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

  2. Custom ASIC share of accelerator spend rises above ~25% on disclosed unit or revenue data (Broadcom and Marvell disclosures are the read). That would mean merchant GPU is losing the architecture argument, not just the price argument.

Valuation conclusion

At an indicative ~$201 against a $245 target we see roughly 22% upside, arrived at by applying 28x to a forward EPS of about $8.80. We deliberately use 28x rather than the 30–40x NVIDIA has historically commanded: the cycle is later, the financing question is unresolved, and a multiple assumption should not be the thing doing the work in a bull case. The consensus target of ~$303 implies 35x, which we think is too generous in the current regime. Our target sits 19% below consensus while our rating is Overweight — that combination is deliberate and honest: we like the risk/reward, we do not endorse the street's multiple.

Sector-specific read:

Data-centre revenue as a share of total, and its sequential growth rate, are the only two numbers that matter. Secondary: gross margin (the pricing-power read), inventory and supply commitments (the forward-demand read), and disclosed CoWoS/HBM allocation, which is the physical constraint on how much NVIDIA can actually ship regardless of demand.

Expectations & earnings reaction pattern

The important shift is that expectations have fallen faster than estimates. A stock at 23x forward does not need a large beat to hold flat — which is a materially different setup from the last two years, when NVIDIA repeatedly beat and fell. The question our standards demand — "what does this stock need to do to go up?" — now has a different answer than it did in June: it needs a customer to reaffirm 2027 capex. It no longer needs to beat its own numbers. That is a change in what the market is rewarding, and noticing it is the point.

Narrative read:

Below the 50-day and only marginally above the 200-day, having given back the entire second quarter. The trend structure is deteriorating and we are explicitly buying into weakness, which our own principles warn about: being right early is indistinguishable from being wrong. The mitigating evidence is that the drawdown is sector-wide (the SOX is in a bear market) and correlated with financing news rather than with anything NVIDIA-specific. If NVIDIA were falling while peers held, we would treat that as information and stand down.

Reminder: a stock with perfect fundamentals that the market has decided to dislike will not go up. Momentum is evidence, not noise.

Management & guidance behaviour

We have not yet built the eight-quarter guided-versus-delivered series that our standards require — the guidance tracker is empty and this is a genuine gap in the analysis rather than a judgement. What is observable: management has consistently guided to revenue it can already see in committed supply, which in a supply-constrained regime produces systematic beats that say more about the constraint than about conservatism. Do not read the beat history as a sandbag factor until we can separate the two. Flagged to Agent B2 to build the series before the next review.

Teaching note

The transferable lesson here is about locating risk correctly in a value chain. When a sector sells off on a single narrative — "AI spending is unsustainable" — the market tends to mark everything down together. But a spending problem is not symmetric: it is bad for whoever must fund the spending and, at worst, merely slower for whoever receives it. The analytical move is to ask, for every name in a selloff, "does this company pay or get paid?" and then check whether the price action distinguishes between the two. When it does not, that is where mispricing lives. Note also the discipline of setting a target below consensus while rating a stock Overweight — a rating expresses risk/reward, and a target expresses your own valuation. Conflating them is how research desks end up marking their targets to the market.
