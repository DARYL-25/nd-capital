Our view

Investment thesis

Micron is the sharpest expression of the central divergence in this market. Operationally, almost nothing has ever looked better: revenue up 346% year-on-year, EPS up more than thirteen-fold, an 80% operating margin, 2026 HBM supply sold out, and third-quarter DRAM contract prices settling higher again after a cumulative year-to-date ASP increase of roughly 340%. Meanwhile the stock trades at 5.3x forward earnings and has fallen ~15% below its 50-day average. A forward multiple that low on rising prices is the market making an explicit statement: these earnings are the peak and they will not persist. That statement may well be correct — it usually is, eventually, in memory — but the market is making it while contract prices are still going up, which is unusually early. We rate this Overweight because the evidence currently contradicts the price, and we hold conviction at Medium rather than High because calling the top of a memory cycle is the single most reliable way for an analyst to destroy capital, in either direction.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

The divergence itself is the finding. Our standing principles say divergences between improving fundamentals and deteriorating price action are where the information is. This is the largest one in our coverage: pricing accelerating, equity de-rating. Only two readings are possible. Either the market is forecasting the cycle turn earlier and better than the pricing data can show — which memory investors are historically quite good at — or forced de-risking in the AI complex is dragging down a name whose earnings are contractually locked for the next several quarters. We think it is materially the second, because the selloff hit Samsung and SK hynix more than 15% in a single session, and moves of that magnitude across three separately-listed companies in different markets are liquidation, not analysis.

Second, HBM changes the cycle's shape and the market is still modelling the old shape. HBM is sold on long-term agreements with qualification barriers, which is structurally different from spot commodity DRAM. It does not eliminate cyclicality; it lengthens and smooths it. A 5.3x multiple is a commodity multiple applied to a business whose mix is shifting away from commodity.

Third — and arguing against ourselves — capacity is being added. All three major players are shifting capacity to HBM and server DRAM, which reduces commodity output today but adds total bits eventually. The bear case does not require demand to fall; it only requires supply to arrive. That is the honest reason this is Medium conviction.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Specific and monitorable. If any of these prints, we change the view in public.

Specific and monitorable. Agent A3 (Thesis Guardian) watches these. Vague conditions are useless.

  1. DRAM contract prices decline quarter-on-quarter. This is the cleanest, highest-frequency disconfirming signal in our entire book. The moment sequential pricing turns negative, the thesis is over — not weakened, over.
  2. HBM qualification slips at a major customer, or 2027 HBM is not sold out on a similar timeline to 2026.
  3. Any of the three major producers announces a capacity expansion materially above current plans — supply discipline is the entire basis of this cycle's economics.

Valuation conclusion

At an indicative ~$823 against a $1,150 target, ~40% upside on 7.4x a forward EPS near $155. Consensus sits at ~$1,522, which we regard as too high — it extrapolates peak earnings at a higher multiple, which is the classic late-cycle error in this sector. Our target is 24% below consensus while our rating is Overweight. The critical framing: applying any multiple to peak cyclical earnings is dangerous. We use 7.4x precisely because it embeds substantial earnings decay. An investor should size this position on the bear case ($450), not the base case.

Sector-specific read:

DRAM and NAND contract ASPs sequentially — this is the number, everything else is commentary. Then: bit shipment growth, HBM revenue as a share of total, HBM sold-out horizon (currently through 2026), inventory days at customers, and industry capex discipline across all three producers.

Expectations & earnings reaction pattern

This is where the analysis gets uncomfortable and where our principles are most useful. A stock at 5.3x forward does not need a beat — it needs the market to believe the earnings are durable. Micron has a documented history of falling on good results when the cycle is suspected of turning. Beating will not be enough. The stock needs a credible 2027 pricing framework, and management may reasonably decline to give one. Investors should expect the shares to trade on contract-price data between reports rather than on the reports themselves.

Narrative read:

Roughly 15% below the 50-day but ~59% above the 200-day — the shape of a parabolic advance correcting, not a trend breaking. That distinction matters. A stock that has tripled and pulls back 15% is behaving normally; the same 15% from below the 200-day would be a different signal entirely. We are watching the 200-day as the level that would change the read. The narrative has turned decisively hostile while the data has not, and our own principles warn that the narrative usually wins over a 6–12 month horizon regardless of fundamentals. We are consciously accepting that risk here, which is precisely why conviction is Medium.

Reminder: a stock with perfect fundamentals that the market has decided to dislike will not go up. Momentum is evidence, not noise.

Management & guidance behaviour

Cyclical management teams that have survived multiple down-cycles tend toward realism rather than promotion, and Micron's disclosure through this up-cycle has been notably measured. The guidance tracker is not yet built (flagged to B2). The most important management variable is capital discipline — memory cycles end when producers add capacity into strength. Watch capex guidance far more closely than earnings guidance; in this sector capex is the guidance.

Alternative data & sentiment

Contract pricing series (DRAM/NAND) are the highest-value alternative dataset in this sector and we are currently reading them from public reporting rather than tracking them systematically. This is our most material data gap. Flagged to Agent C2 as the top priority.

Standing caveat: social and alternative data is noisy, reflexive, and frequently a contrarian indicator at extremes. Where we have not measured something, we say so rather than inferring it.

Teaching note

This is the most instructive name in the book, for two reasons.

First, low multiples on cyclical peaks are a warning, not a bargain. A 5.3x forward P/E does not mean cheap — it means the market believes the E is about to fall. The correct response is never "look how cheap," it is "what does the market think earnings normalise to, and do I have a better estimate?" Work the denominator, not the ratio.

Second, when data and price disagree, resist resolving the tension too quickly. The temptation is to declare the market wrong (satisfying) or to defer to it (safe). The professional move is to identify the single observable that would settle it — here, sequential contract prices — and then commit in advance to what you will do when it prints. We have done that in section 5. Deciding your disconfirming evidence before you need it is most of what separates a process from an opinion.
