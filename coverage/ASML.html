Our view

Investment thesis

ASML is the only supplier of extreme-ultraviolet lithography systems in the world. There is no second source, no credible entrant, and no path to leading-edge logic or advanced DRAM that does not pass through its order book. Revenue is growing 21.3% at a 37% operating margin, and the memory capex cycle now underway adds a demand vector that is largely independent of the logic cycle. At 36.4x forward earnings it is not cheap — monopolies rarely are — and the multiple is the main reason conviction is Medium rather than High. The bear case is concentration: a handful of customers, long lead times, and full exposure to export-control policy, which has already cost ASML access to parts of the Chinese market.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

The memory capex cycle is a second demand engine and it is arriving now. Most ASML analysis is framed around leading-edge logic — TSMC's node roadmap, Intel's ambitions. But with DRAM contract prices up sharply and all three memory producers shifting capacity toward HBM and server DRAM, memory-driven equipment demand is rising at the same time. Two demand cycles overlapping in the same order book is not the historical norm for this business, and order intake is lumpy enough that it may not be visible in reported revenue for several quarters.

Second, order intake matters far more than revenue here and it is a leading indicator of the whole industry. ASML's bookings tell you what fabs intend to build 18–24 months out. That makes it one of the most valuable forward-looking datasets in technology, and it is under-used outside of specialist coverage.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Specific and monitorable. If any of these prints, we change the view in public.

Specific and monitorable. Agent A3 (Thesis Guardian) watches these. Vague conditions are useless.

  1. Quarterly net bookings decline for two consecutive quarters — the cleanest leading indicator of a fab capex downturn.
  2. A major customer delays or cancels a High-NA order, indicating the next node transition is being pushed out.
  3. Export controls extend to further restrict the installed base or servicing revenue, which is the high-margin annuity underneath the systems business.

Valuation conclusion

42x a forward EPS of about $44.80 gives $1,875, roughly 15% upside. Consensus is ~$2,140. We are 12% below, applying a discount for export-control exposure that we regard as genuinely un-modellable. The honest framing: at 36x forward you are paying a monopoly multiple for a monopoly, and the return comes from earnings growth rather than from any re-rating. There is no multiple expansion in our base case.

ASML's results are noisy because revenue recognition on multi-hundred-million-dollar systems creates lumpy quarters. The market has learned to look through revenue to bookings, which means what this stock needs to go up is a bookings number, not an EPS number. Management & guidance behaviour

Conservative guidance culture with high disclosure quality — ASML publishes bookings, which most equipment vendors do not, and that transparency is a genuine analytical asset. Long-range capacity planning has been broadly credible across cycles. Guidance tracker not built; flagged to B2.

Teaching note

ASML teaches the difference between a good business and a good investment, and how to hold both thoughts at once. It is arguably the best business in the semiconductor supply chain — a true monopoly on an essential technology. That fact is fully known and fully priced at 36x forward. Our Overweight is therefore not "this is a great company" (everyone knows) but "earnings will compound faster than the market expects because two capex cycles are overlapping." The rating rests on the incremental insight, not on the quality.

Second, note the leading-indicator hierarchy: bookings lead revenue, revenue leads earnings, earnings lead the reported multiple. For any company you cover, find the earliest observable point in that chain and track it. ASML bookings lead the entire semiconductor capex cycle by roughly 18 months, which makes them useful for names far beyond ASML itself.
