Our view

Investment thesis

Qualcomm is statistically the cheapest name in our coverage on forward earnings at 14x, with a 2.37% dividend yield and a PEG of 0.63. It is also the only name in our coverage with declining revenue (−4% year-on-year) and EPS (−23%). The cause is well known: Apple has been progressively replacing Qualcomm modems with its own silicon, removing a large and highly profitable revenue stream on a known schedule. The street reflects this with 23 hold ratings against 12 bullish — the most bearish distribution in our coverage. We rate Neutral rather than Underweight because the valuation genuinely does compensate for a good deal of this, and the automotive and IoT diversification is real. But cheap and shrinking is the classic value-trap signature, and we are not willing to call it value until revenue stabilises.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

We do not have an information edge here — the Apple modem transition is the most telegraphed event in the semiconductor sector. The framing point worth making is that Qualcomm is the control experiment for the entire edge-AI thesis. If on-device AI genuinely drives a handset replacement cycle and richer silicon content, Qualcomm is the most direct beneficiary and its revenue should stabilise despite the Apple loss. If Qualcomm's revenue keeps declining, the edge-AI upgrade-cycle thesis is not working — which has direct implications for how we think about Apple.

That makes this name analytically valuable well beyond its own risk/reward: it is a read on a theme, and we will watch it as one.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Valuation conclusion

14x a forward EPS of about $10.57 gives $150 — roughly flat. Consensus is ~$196. We are 24% below. The valuation is genuinely low and genuinely justified, which is what a value trap looks like from the inside. The distinguishing test between "cheap" and "trap" is the direction of revenue, and it is currently negative. We will revisit immediately on two consecutive quarters of revenue growth.

Teaching note

This name teaches how to tell cheap from a value trap. The test is not the multiple, it is the direction of the denominator. A low P/E on growing earnings is cheap; a low P/E on shrinking earnings is a warning that the market has correctly identified a business in decline. The practical rule: before buying anything on a low multiple, require two consecutive quarters of stabilising or growing revenue. You will miss the exact bottom. You will also avoid the much larger category of things that looked cheap all the way down.

Second, note the idea of a name as a read on a theme. Qualcomm's revenue trajectory is the cleanest public test of whether on-device AI is driving a real upgrade cycle. Even if you never own it, tracking it tells you something about Apple. Building a small set of these "indicator" names — companies whose results settle a debate that affects your bigger positions — is one of the highest-leverage habits in research.
