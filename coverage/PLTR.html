Our view

Investment thesis

Palantir is a genuinely exceptional business — 84.7% revenue growth, a 46.2% operating margin and a 43.7% net margin are an extraordinary combination at this scale, and the government and commercial franchises are both real. The problem is arithmetic: the stock trades at 56.5x trailing sales and 84x forward earnings after falling 41% from its high. Our valuation discipline does not have a defensible bridge to those multiples on any reasonable set of assumptions. Reports this week, which makes the timing of this call binary and means position sizing matters far more than the target. We want to be unambiguous that this is a valuation call against a superb operating business, not a criticism of the company.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

We do not claim to see something the market cannot. Palantir's quality is widely recognised, and so is its valuation. The observation worth recording is structural: Palantir has been the purest sentiment proxy for AI software, and sentiment proxies de-rate before and further than fundamentals-driven names when a theme turns. It fell 41% from its high while revenue grew 85%. That is the market removing a sentiment premium, not revising a forecast, and there is no principle establishing where such a removal stops. A stock with no valuation anchor on the way up has none on the way down either.

Second, the retail ownership base is analytically relevant. Institutional ownership of 62.4% is low for a company of this size, implying an unusually large retail float. Retail-heavy ownership tends to produce both stronger momentum and sharper reversals, which is consistent with the observed price history and argues for wider position-sizing bands in both directions.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Valuation conclusion

60x a forward EPS of about $1.46 gives $95, roughly 23% downside. Consensus is ~$182, and notably the street is split with 10 holds, a sell and a strong sell — an unusually unenthusiastic distribution for a stock this widely owned, which suggests professional investors share our discomfort with the multiple. At 56.5x trailing sales, Palantir would need to grow revenue 5.6-fold, holding margins, merely to reach a 10x sales multiple. At 84.7% growth that is 2.8 years of flawless compounding just to become expensive by normal standards. That is what you are paying for today.

US commercial revenue growth and customer count — the part of the story that justifies any premium at all. Then: total contract value and remaining deal value, net dollar retention, government versus commercial mix, operating margin, and stock-based compensation as a percentage of revenue, which is material for this name and understates the true cost of the earnings.

Expectations & earnings reaction pattern

Very high earnings risk into an imminent report. At 84x forward, this stock needs a large beat and a raise simply to hold its level — and it has previously fallen on results that beat. Expectations are extremely elevated relative to any reasonable bar. The asymmetry into the print is unattractive in our view, though we acknowledge it has been unattractive for some time and the stock rose anyway.

Management & guidance behaviour

Highly promotional communication style — management engages in public advocacy for the stock in a way few peers do. That is a genuine consideration: it attracts a shareholder base that trades on narrative, which increases volatility in both directions. Operating execution has nevertheless been excellent and should not be dismissed because the communication style is unusual. Guidance tracker not built; flagged to B2 ahead of this week's report.

Teaching note

The reverse-multiple test in the valuation section is the technique to take from this name, and it is the discipline our standards care most about. Do not stop at "56x sales is expensive" — that is an adjective, not an analysis. Convert the multiple into an operational requirement: to reach a 10x sales multiple while holding margins, revenue must grow 5.6-fold, which at 84.7% annual growth takes 2.8 years of flawless execution — and that only gets you to expensive. Now you have something falsifiable, and you can ask whether the company has ever done that, whether the market is large enough to permit it, and what happens if growth decelerates to merely excellent.

The second lesson is about separating the company from the stock, which our principles list explicitly. Palantir is a superb business. It can be a superb business and a poor investment simultaneously, and holding both thoughts without letting one contaminate the other is one of the harder disciplines in this work. Most analytical errors on names like this come from resolving that tension prematurely in one direction or the other.
