Our view

Investment thesis

Broadcom is the primary beneficiary of the one structural change the AI-compute market is actually undergoing: the largest buyers of accelerators want to stop paying merchant margins and are moving to custom silicon. Revenue is growing 48% year-on-year at a 49% operating margin, and the forward P/E of 20.8x against a trailing 65x tells you consensus expects a very large earnings ramp as design wins move into volume production. A PEG of 0.44 is the lowest in our coverage. The bear case — that custom ASIC programmes are lumpy, customer-concentrated and vulnerable to a single hyperscaler changing its mind — is entirely fair, and it is why we hold this at High rather than very high conviction. But the direction of travel is not in dispute: in a cycle where the buyers' central problem has become cost, the vendor that lowers their cost takes share.

The edge, if there is one

What the market is missing

The market is treating custom silicon as a threat to NVIDIA and forgetting that it is a business for someone. Almost all of the analytical energy in this sector goes into debating how much share ASICs take from merchant GPU. Far less goes into the fact that Broadcom is the counterparty on the other side of that shift, with the infrastructure-software franchise (VMware) funding the R&D and smoothing the cyclicality.

Second, the trailing multiple is actively misleading and we suspect it is doing real work in screens. Broadcom screens at 65x trailing — expensive — and 21x forward. Any systematic process ranking on trailing P/E will mis-sort this name badly right now. That is a mechanical source of mispricing, and mechanical sources are the most reliable kind.

Third, networking is the quiet half of the story. Broadcom's merchant switching silicon is simultaneously taking share from Arista's integrated systems. We are Overweight AVGO and Neutral ANET on the same underlying observation, which is a cleaner expression of the view than either leg alone.

Held to account

What would prove us wrong

Specific and monitorable. If any of these prints, we change the view in public.

Specific and monitorable. Agent A3 (Thesis Guardian) watches these. Vague conditions are useless.

  1. A major hyperscaler publicly moves a custom programme to another vendor or in-house. Customer concentration is the core fragility; one defection changes the earnings ramp materially.
  2. The forward-EPS ramp is pushed out two quarters or more in guidance — the gap between 65x trailing and 21x forward only closes if the earnings actually arrive on schedule.
  3. Merchant GPU pricing falls far enough that the cost argument for custom silicon weakens. Watch NVIDIA gross margin as the leading indicator here.

Valuation conclusion

We apply 26x to a forward EPS of about $18.70 for a $480 target, roughly 23% above the indicative price. Consensus sits at ~$528. Our discount reflects customer concentration, which we think deserves an explicit haircut rather than a mention in the risk section. On EV/EBITDA the name looks expensive at 44.8x, which is the trailing-figure distortion again — the whole valuation case rests on the forward ramp being real, and an investor who does not believe the ramp should not own this at any of these multiples.

Sector-specific read:

AI revenue as a disclosed line and its sequential growth; custom accelerator design wins moving from "announced" to "in production" (announcements are worth far less); infrastructure-software renewal rates and pricing; and switching-silicon share versus integrated systems vendors.

Expectations & earnings reaction pattern

The gap between 65x trailing and 21x forward means expectations for the earnings ramp are already high even though the headline multiple looks moderate. This stock does not need a beat on the current quarter; it needs confirmation that the ramp is on schedule. A reiterated ramp with an in-line quarter is a better outcome for the stock than a beat with a pushed-out ramp — a useful reminder that "beat" and "good result" are not the same event.

Reminder: a stock with perfect fundamentals that the market has decided to dislike will not go up. Momentum is evidence, not noise.

Management & guidance behaviour

Broadcom's management has a long-standing pattern of guiding conservatively and delivering above — the classic sandbag profile — but we have not yet built the quantified eight-quarter series and will not assign a sandbag factor without it. Flagged to B2. Capital allocation is disciplined and shareholder-oriented. The acquisition record is strong on integration and margin extraction, weaker on customer goodwill; the VMware repricing generated real customer hostility, which is a durable-revenue risk that does not show up in current numbers.

Teaching note

Two lessons. First, always check whether a multiple is trailing or forward before forming an opinion — Broadcom looks like one of the most expensive names in the sector on one basis and one of the cheapest on the other, and the entire disagreement about the stock lives in that gap. Second, and more subtly: when you identify a structural shift, ask who is on the other side of it. The market spent two years debating what custom silicon does to NVIDIA and comparatively little time on who books the revenue. Every threat to one company is a business for another, and the second-order beneficiary is usually less crowded than the first-order victim is shorted.
