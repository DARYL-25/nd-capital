Robotics & Physical AI

AI that acts in the physical world. Genuinely large if it works, and currently supported by demonstrations rather than revenue.

Stage · Emerging — narrative only

Physical AI covers humanoid robots, autonomous systems and embodied models — applying learned policies to machines that act in the world rather than to text and images. If it works at scale the addressable market is larger than anything in software, because it addresses labour rather than tooling. It is currently a research and demonstration field with negligible listed revenue, and we hold no position.

Be honest about lateness

Where we are in the lifecycle

Be honest about lateness. Most money in thematic investing is lost by arriving at "Crowded."

Emerging, narrative-driven. Capital is flowing, demonstrations are improving rapidly, and commercial deployment at scale is not yet visible. This is the stage at which valuations are set entirely by imagination, which can persist for a long time and is not analysable with the tools we use.

Second- and third-order beneficiaries

This is where the non-obvious money is. Who supplies the supplier?

If physical AI does reach scale, the second-order beneficiaries are precision components — motors, harmonic drives, force sensors, bearings — which are concentrated in Japanese and European industrial listings outside our coverage. That is worth noting now, before the theme is named, because the components layer has historically been where the durable margins in automation sit.

What the market is rewarding right now

Narrative only. No revenue-based discrimination is possible yet, which is precisely why we do not have a position.

The bear case for the theme itself

Argued at full strength: robotics has produced compelling demonstrations for four decades without producing general-purpose commercial deployment. The gap between a controlled demo and a machine that works reliably in an uncontrolled environment for years has repeatedly proven larger than expected, and the failure modes are physical and expensive rather than merely embarrassing. Unit economics are unproven, and the labour costs being targeted are frequently lower than the fully-loaded cost of the machine plus its maintenance. Most likely, this theme takes a decade longer than its advocates expect — which is not a reason to ignore it, but is a decisive reason not to pay for it today.

ETF expressions
