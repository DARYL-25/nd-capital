Advanced Packaging

Chips stopped getting faster by shrinking alone. Now they get faster by being stacked — and stacking capacity is the industry's real gate.

Stage · Accelerating

As transistor scaling has slowed, performance gains have shifted toward how die are assembled — chiplets, 2.5D and 3D stacking, and through-silicon vias. Advanced packaging capacity, and TSMC's CoWoS in particular, has repeatedly been the binding constraint on how many AI accelerators actually reach a rack, ahead of both wafer supply and demand. It is one of the least-discussed and most consequential bottlenecks in the entire AI supply chain.

Be honest about lateness

Where we are in the lifecycle

Be honest about lateness. Most money in thematic investing is lost by arriving at "Crowded."

Accelerating. Capacity is being expanded aggressively and remains tight; pricing power sits firmly with the supplier. The theme is not yet consensus in the way that GPUs are, largely because it is technical, unglamorous and does not map to a single listed pure-play — which is precisely why it stays under-analysed.

Evidence for this staging: packaging capacity allocation has repeatedly determined accelerator shipment volumes; expansion announcements continue; and process control content per wafer is rising as stacking complexity increases.

Second- and third-order beneficiaries

This is where the non-obvious money is. Who supplies the supplier?

Process control is the sharpest second-order play and the economics are non-linear. When a defect is discovered after stacking, it destroys several good die rather than one. The economic value of finding it earlier therefore rises faster than the number of steps does. KLA's addressable content per wafer grows structurally as packaging complexity increases, independent of wafer volumes — a content story rather than a volume story, which is the more durable kind.

Third-order: substrate and materials suppliers, mostly Japanese and Taiwanese listings outside our current coverage, which is a genuine gap in the book rather than a deliberate exclusion.

What the market is rewarding right now

Capacity ownership. In a constrained market the market pays for tonnage, not technology roadmaps. TSMC's disclosed CoWoS expansion is followed far more closely by the industry than by generalist investors — one of the clearer information asymmetries available in a widely-covered sector.

The bear case for the theme itself

Argued at full strength: bottlenecks attract capital, and capital eliminates bottlenecks. Packaging capacity is being expanded by everyone simultaneously — TSMC, the OSATs, and the memory producers. When it arrives together, the pricing power that makes this theme attractive disappears, and the equipment vendors that benefited from the expansion face the classic post-build-out air pocket. Additionally, the theme has no clean listed expression, which means most of the return accrues to companies where packaging is a minority of revenue — so even a correct call on the theme may be substantially diluted by the time it reaches a portfolio.
