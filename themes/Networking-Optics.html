Networking & Optics

Training clusters are networks first and computers second. The question is whether integrated systems or merchant silicon capture the value.

Stage · Consensus

Large AI training clusters are limited as much by how fast their accelerators can talk to each other as by the accelerators themselves, which has made high-speed switching and optical interconnect a first-order part of the build-out. The open question is not whether networking demand grows — it plainly does — but who captures it: integrated systems vendors like Arista, or merchant switching silicon from Broadcom feeding hyperscaler-designed white-box hardware. We think the economics favour merchant silicon over time, and we express that as Overweight AVGO alongside Neutral ANET.

Second- and third-order beneficiaries

This is where the non-obvious money is. Who supplies the supplier?

Optical interconnect is the under-covered layer. As cluster sizes grow, the proportion of interconnect that must be optical rather than copper rises, because copper reach limits bite at scale. Most optical module manufacturing sits outside US listings, so the theme is difficult to express and correspondingly under-analysed — Marvell is the most accessible listed exposure and it comes bundled with a custom-silicon story we like considerably less.

What the market is rewarding right now

Share defence. For Arista specifically, the market is watching gross margin as the test of whether share is being held on merit or bought with price. For Broadcom, the market wants disclosed AI networking revenue.

The bear case for the theme itself

Argued at full strength — against our own positioning: merchant silicon has been "about to commoditise networking" for two decades and Arista has grown share and margin throughout. AI cluster networking is genuinely harder than general datacentre switching — congestion control, lossless fabrics and cluster-scale telemetry are software problems, and software is precisely where Arista's moat sits. White-box works well when the workload is simple; AI training is not simple. It is entirely possible that the complexity of AI networking strengthens the integrated vendor rather than weakening it, in which case our AVGO-over-ANET pair is wrong on the leg we were most confident about.
