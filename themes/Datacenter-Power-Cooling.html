Datacenter Power & Cooling

The unglamorous physical constraint. A rack you cannot cool is a rack you cannot deploy, whatever the chip supply looks like.

Stage · Accelerating

Modern AI racks draw an order of magnitude more power, and reject an order of magnitude more heat, than the general-purpose infrastructure that datacentres were designed around. That has made power distribution, liquid cooling and grid interconnection genuine gating constraints on deployment rather than back-office considerations. The theme sits mostly in industrial sector codes, which means it is systematically missed by anyone screening technology — one of the more durable structural reasons for mispricing.

Be honest about lateness

Where we are in the lifecycle

Be honest about lateness. Most money in thematic investing is lost by arriving at "Crowded."

Accelerating, and earlier in its lifecycle than the silicon themes. Liquid cooling adoption is still in its early innings; grid interconnection queues in several major markets now run to multiple years, which is a constraint that cannot be relieved by capital alone.

Evidence for this staging: cooling and switchgear lead times have lengthened; retrofit projects are skills-constrained as well as capital-constrained; and interconnection queues are lengthening rather than clearing.

Second- and third-order beneficiaries

This is where the non-obvious money is. Who supplies the supplier?

Grid interconnection is the deepest and least-owned constraint in the entire AI complex. It is not generation capacity — it is the queue to connect. Multi-year interconnection backlogs in key markets mean that even a fully-funded, fully-supplied datacentre may not be able to energise. The listed expressions sit in utilities and independent power producers, which is outside our current coverage and is a genuine gap we should close.

Third-order: the skilled-trades constraint. Liquid cooling retrofits need electricians and pipefitters with specific qualifications, and that labour pool cannot be expanded on a capex timescale. This is not investable directly but it is a real reason to be sceptical of aggressive deployment schedules.

What the market is rewarding right now

Backlog conversion, not order intake. The market has heard the announcements; what it now wants is delivered revenue. In capital-equipment businesses the orders line is the forward disclosure, and book-to-bill is read before earnings.

The bear case for the theme itself

Argued at full strength: these are industrial businesses with industrial economics — Vertiv's 20.4% operating margin is respectable for electrical equipment and unremarkable next to anything in semiconductors. Competition is real, barriers are moderate, and the current multiple (37.6x forward) assumes the present pricing environment persists. If datacentre construction slows, this theme has none of the contractual protections of a sold-out memory order book: it is project-based revenue that stops when the projects stop. Additionally, a bottleneck that is visible and profitable attracts entrants, and electrical equipment has far lower barriers than semiconductor manufacturing.
