AI Software Monetization

The layer where AI is supposed to become revenue rather than cost. So far the evidence is thin and the multiples are not.

Stage · Crowded — breaking

This is the layer where the AI build-out is meant to be repaid: applications and platforms that convert model capability into recurring revenue. It remains the most expensive corner of the market even after a severe drawdown — Palantir trades at roughly 56x trailing sales after falling 41% from its high — while the incumbent enterprise software names have de-rated hard on fears that agentic AI destroys seat-based licensing. The gap between what this layer is priced to deliver and what it has demonstrably delivered is the widest in our coverage.

Be honest about lateness

Where we are in the lifecycle

Be honest about lateness. Most money in thematic investing is lost by arriving at "Crowded."

Crowded and breaking. This is the classic late-stage configuration: extreme multiples, retail-heavy ownership in the highest-multiple names, a compelling narrative, and prices that have begun falling while fundamentals still look good.

Evidence for this staging: Palantir at 56x sales after a 41% drawdown; Salesforce de-rated to 13.5x forward on a disruption thesis not yet visible in its numbers; extreme valuation dispersion within the same theme. When the same theme contains both the most expensive and among the cheapest names in a market, the theme label has stopped carrying information.

Second- and third-order beneficiaries

This is where the non-obvious money is. Who supplies the supplier?

The data and integration layer is where the durable value probably sits, and it is mostly private. Models are increasingly commoditised; proprietary data, workflow integration and compliance are not. That argues for incumbents with entrenched enterprise positions over AI-native challengers — which is the core of our Salesforce Overweight and is a genuinely contrarian position within this theme.

Third-order: the services and implementation layer. Every enterprise AI deployment requires substantial integration work, and the listed consultancies capture some of it — outside our current coverage.

What the market is rewarding right now

Proven revenue, not demonstrations. The market's tolerance for agent demos without revenue attached has ended. This shift happened during the current earnings season and it is why the theme's highest-multiple names have de-rated hardest.

The bear case for the theme itself

Argued at full strength: the AI software layer has to generate enough revenue to justify roughly $725bn of annual infrastructure spending, and there is no public evidence that it is close to doing so. Aggregate AI application revenue remains a small fraction of aggregate AI capex, and the gap is being financed by balance sheets rather than by customers. If agentic AI genuinely reduces seat counts, incumbent software revenue declines while the cost base does not, and the sector faces both structural revenue pressure and a de-rating simultaneously. The most likely outcome is that model capability is commoditised, application prices fall, and value accrues to customers rather than to any listed vendor — which would be excellent for the economy and poor for this theme.
