Custom Silicon & ASIC

The largest buyers of accelerators are trying to stop paying merchant margins. That shift has a supplier, and the supplier is listed.

Stage · Accelerating

Hyperscalers designing their own AI accelerators — Google's TPU, Amazon's Trainium, and the custom programmes Broadcom and Marvell build for named customers — represent the most credible structural challenge to merchant GPU economics. The motivation is unambiguous and has strengthened considerably now that the cost of AI infrastructure has become the market's central concern rather than an afterthought. Most analysis frames this as a threat to NVIDIA and neglects the fact that it is a rapidly growing business for whoever builds the silicon.

Be honest about lateness

Where we are in the lifecycle

Be honest about lateness. Most money in thematic investing is lost by arriving at "Crowded."

Accelerating. Design wins are moving from announcement into production, which is the phase where revenue actually appears. The theme is well known qualitatively and poorly quantified — almost nobody has a defensible estimate of custom silicon's share of total accelerator spend, which is itself informative about how early this is.

Evidence for this staging: Broadcom's revenue growing 48% with a 49% operating margin; Alphabet's internal deployment scaling; the forward-versus-trailing multiple gap at Broadcom (20.8x vs 65x) indicating a large expected earnings ramp not yet delivered.

Second- and third-order beneficiaries

This is where the non-obvious money is. Who supplies the supplier?

The in-house programmes are the least visible expression, because they show up as an absent cost rather than as revenue. Alphabet's TPU never appears as a line item; it appears as a lower cost per unit of compute than a competitor buying merchant accelerators. That is invisible to any screen and difficult to model, which is exactly why it is under-priced. When a sector's central problem becomes cost, the company with a structurally lower cost curve should re-rate relative to peers — and Alphabet de-rated alongside them.

Third-order: the design services and IP layer, where much of the engineering is actually done, sits largely in private and non-US-listed companies outside our coverage.

What the market is rewarding right now

Production, not announcements. The market has learned to discount design-win press releases heavily. What moves these names now is disclosed revenue from programmes already shipping — which is the correct discipline and a healthy sign for the theme's maturity.

The bear case for the theme itself

Argued at full strength: custom silicon programmes are lumpy, deeply customer-concentrated, and reversible. A single hyperscaler changing architecture, delaying a generation, or moving a programme in-house removes a material share of a vendor's earnings ramp with little warning. Custom accelerators also consistently lag merchant GPUs on software maturity, and the total cost of ownership advantage narrows considerably once engineering effort is properly accounted for — a cost that sits with the customer and never appears in the vendor's numbers. There is a real possibility that custom silicon plateaus at a modest share of total accelerator spend, in which case Broadcom's forward multiple embeds a ramp that never fully arrives.
